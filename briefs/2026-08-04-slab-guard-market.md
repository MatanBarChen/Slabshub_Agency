## Demand drivers
- Corner/edge protection in shipping and handling (primary functional driver).
- Display aesthetic / color personalization — bright colors and glitter/metallic finishes sell at a premium (GradedGuard limited editions run 2x standard price).
- Collector identity / color-coding a collection by set, player, or type.
- Stacking capability is a differentiator some brands trade off against fit snugness.

## Buyer complaints (category-wide)
- Fit too tight → hard to remove without prying (GradedGuard review); backward-install confusion (which side faces front).
- Case itself scratches/scuffs from drops — protects the card but visibly damages the guard, seen as a cosmetic issue by resellers.
- Bare/no protective packaging on shipment (GenZGaming review).

## Recommended positioning for SlabsHub
**Primary angle — "protect the slab you just bought" bundle attach.** We're the only seller in this landscape who also sells the graded slab itself — no competitor found here does both. Pitch as a $3–5 checkout add-on ("you just paid $200+ for this grade — protect the case for a coffee") rather than a standalone accessory purchase. This converts far better than any competitor's cold-traffic single-SKU listing.
**Backup angle — collector color-coding / display aesthetic**, leaning on the glitter/ombre line as the "limited/premium" tier (mirrors GradedGuard's metallic upsell, which sold at ~2x base price).

## Suggested price architecture
- Solid-color single: $4–5 (above GenZGaming's rock-bottom $2, well below GradedGuard's $10 — justified by bundle context, not needing to compete standalone)

- Glitter/ombre premium line: $7–9 single (well under GradedGuard's $18–22 limited edition, still reads premium vs solid line)
- Bundle add-on price (attached to a slab purchase): $3, lower than standalone singles — the discount IS the hook
- Free-shipping threshold: set lower than category norm ($35–40) since bumper-only orders will be low-AOV; bundle orders clear it automatically via the slab price
